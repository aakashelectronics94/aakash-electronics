# Setting Up an Engineering College Electronics Lab: Complete Component Checklist

Setting up an electronics lab for an engineering college, whether from scratch or a yearly restock, involves more moving parts than most procurement checklists account for. This guide walks through the categories a well-equipped lab needs, organized the way a purchase committee would actually plan the budget.

## 1. Development Boards

The core of any hands-on electronics or embedded systems curriculum.

- Arduino Uno/Mega, for foundational digital and analog I/O coursework
- Raspberry Pi 5, for final-year projects involving image processing, ML, or networking
- ESP32/ESP8266, for IoT electives and robotics club use
- Basic microcontroller trainer kits for first-year students

## 2. Breadboarding & Prototyping Essentials

- Breadboard kits (half-size and full-size)
- Jumper wire sets (male-male, male-female, female-female)
- Breadboard power supply modules
- Prototyping/perfboards for semi-permanent builds

## 3. Sensor Kits

A general-purpose sensor kit covering the most commonly used modules saves individual purchase orders later:

- Ultrasonic distance sensors
- IR proximity/obstacle sensors
- Temperature and humidity sensors (DHT11/DHT22)
- Accelerometer/gyroscope modules
- Light-dependent resistors (LDR) and photodiodes
- Soil moisture and gas sensors (useful for agri-tech and environmental electives)

## 4. Robotics Kits

For robotics coursework and clubs:

- Chassis kits with motors and wheels
- Motor driver modules (L298N or equivalent)
- Servo motors for actuator-based projects
- Line-following and obstacle-avoidance sensor sets

## 5. Test & Measurement Equipment

Every lab bench needs reliable test gear. This is where budget often gets tight, but it's not a category to skimp on:

- Digital multimeters (one per bench minimum)
- Bench power supplies (variable voltage/current)
- Oscilloscopes (shared resource, at least one per lab section)
- Function generators for signal-based experiments

## 6. Soldering Stations & Tools

- Adjustable-temperature soldering stations
- Soldering accessories: solder wire, flux, desoldering pump
- Wire strippers, cutters, and precision screwdrivers
- Safety equipment: fume extractors or ventilation, safety glasses

## 7. Passive & Active Components (Bulk Stock)

Every lab needs a running stock of consumables that get used up every semester:

- Resistors, capacitors, and inductors across common value ranges
- Diodes, transistors, and basic ICs (op-amps, timers like the 555)
- LEDs in various colors and sizes
- Connector sets (headers, terminal blocks)

## 8. Wires & Cables

- Hook-up wire in multiple gauges and colors
- Ribbon cables for multi-pin connections
- USB cables (for programming boards)

## Budgeting Tip: Buy the Full List From One Supplier

Splitting a lab setup across multiple vendors, one for boards, another for sensors, a third for tools, multiplies purchase orders, delivery delays, and reconciliation work for your procurement office. A single supplier who can quote the entire list as one document, with GST invoicing and volume pricing built in, is usually both cheaper and dramatically simpler to process through a college purchase committee.

## Restocking Every Semester

Consumables (resistors, wires, solder, breadboards) run out every semester regardless of how well the initial setup was planned. Setting up a standing relationship with a supplier for recurring restock orders, rather than treating every semester as a fresh procurement cycle, saves significant administrative time.
