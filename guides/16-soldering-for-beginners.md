# Soldering for Beginners: Equipment, Technique and Common Mistakes

Soldering is the skill that takes a project from a loose breadboard to something that lasts. It is easy to learn, but a few bad habits cause most beginner problems. This guide covers what to buy, how to solder a clean joint, and the mistakes to avoid.

## What You Need

**1. Soldering iron or station**
- A **25W to 40W** iron is enough for most through-hole work.
- A **temperature-controlled station** is better, because it holds the tip at a steady temperature and gives you more control.
- Choose a tip shape you can use for both small and larger joints. A small conical or chisel tip works for most boards.

**2. Solder wire**
- Common electronics solder is a **tin-lead alloy (for example 60/40 or 63/37)** with a rosin flux core. It melts at a lower temperature and flows easily, which suits beginners.
- **Lead-free solder** is also available. It needs a higher temperature and is slightly harder to work with.
- A thin wire, around 0.8mm, suits most component work.

**3. Flux**
Flux cleans the metal surfaces and helps solder flow. Most solder wire has flux inside, but extra flux paste or pen helps with difficult joints and desoldering.

**4. Supporting tools**
- Iron stand with a brass wool or damp sponge cleaner
- Side cutters for trimming leads
- Desoldering pump or solder wick for fixing mistakes
- Tweezers and a small vice or "helping hands" to hold work
- A multimeter to check joints and connections

## Setting Up

1. Work in a **well-ventilated** area.
2. Place the iron in its stand, away from papers and cables.
3. Switch on and allow time to heat. For tin-lead solder, a tip temperature in the region of **320°C to 350°C** is typical. Lead-free work usually needs somewhat higher.
4. **Tin the tip:** touch a little solder to the clean tip so it looks shiny. A clean, tinned tip transfers heat well.

## How to Solder a Through-Hole Joint

1. **Insert the component** and bend its leads slightly so it stays in place.
2. **Heat the joint, not the solder.** Touch the iron tip to both the pad and the lead at the same time.
3. **Count one to two seconds,** then feed solder to the joint (not the iron tip).
4. **Let the solder flow** around the lead and cover the pad, forming a small smooth cone.
5. **Remove the solder first,** then the iron.
6. **Keep still** for a moment while the joint cools.
7. **Trim the lead** just above the joint.

The whole joint should take only two to four seconds. Longer than that risks damaging the pad or the component.

## What a Good Joint Looks Like

- **Shiny and smooth** with a concave, volcano-like shape
- The solder has **flowed onto both the pad and the lead**
- No gaps, spikes or blobs

## What a Bad Joint Looks Like

- **Dull, grainy or cracked:** this is a **cold joint.** The solder did not melt properly or moved while cooling. Reheat and add a little fresh solder.
- **A round ball sitting on top:** the solder did not wet the pad. Clean the surfaces and use flux.
- **A bridge between two pads:** excess solder. Remove it with solder wick.
- **A pad that has lifted:** too much heat or force. This usually needs a repair.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- **Applying solder to the iron tip** instead of the joint, which leaves flux burnt and the joint poor.
- **Using too little heat or too much time.** Both cause problems. Aim for a quick, confident joint.
- **Dirty tip.** Wipe and re-tin often.
- **Too much solder.** A small amount is enough.
- **Moving the part while it cools.** This causes cold joints.
- **Overheating sensitive parts.** Transistors, LEDs and ICs can be damaged by long heating. Use an IC socket for chips so you never solder them directly.
- **Wrong polarity.** Check diodes, LEDs, electrolytic capacitors and ICs before soldering.

## Safety Basics

- **Never touch the tip.** It is very hot, and the stand is there for a reason.
- **Ventilate.** Flux fumes are irritating, so use a small fan to move fumes away from your face.
- **Wash your hands** after handling solder, especially tin-lead solder, and before eating.
- **Keep it away from children** and flammable items.
- **Unplug the iron** when finished.
- **Wear eye protection** when trimming leads, which can fly off.

## Practice Before Real Projects

Practise on a cheap piece of perfboard with spare resistors and wire. Aim for ten good joints in a row before soldering a real project.

## Fixing Mistakes

- **Desoldering pump:** heat the joint and suck up the molten solder.
- **Solder wick:** place on the joint, heat it, and let it absorb the solder.
- Remove the old solder fully, then re-solder with fresh solder and flux.
