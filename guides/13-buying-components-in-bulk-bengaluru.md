# How to Buy Electronic Components in Bulk in Bengaluru: A Step-by-Step Guide

Buying 10 resistors and buying 10,000 are different problems. Bulk purchases need planning, because a mistake in a large order costs money, time and production delays. This guide walks through the process for businesses and institutions buying components in volume in Bengaluru.

If you are a manufacturing unit looking for a longer-term supplier, also read our [guide to sourcing components for manufacturing units](guide.html?slug=sourcing-electronic-components-manufacturing-units-india#sourcing-electronic-components-manufacturing-units-india).

## Step 1: Prepare a Clean Bill of Materials (BOM)

A good BOM is the foundation of every bulk order. For each line include:
- **Part number or exact description** (for example, "Metal film resistor 10kΩ 1/4W 1%")
- **Package or form factor** (through-hole, SMD 0805, TO-220, and so on)
- **Quantity per unit** and **total quantity** you need
- **Acceptable alternatives**, if any
- **Brand preference**, if it matters for your product

A BOM that says only "10k resistor" forces the supplier to guess, and wrong guesses become wrong deliveries.

## Step 2: Decide the Right Quantities

- **Add a spares margin.** For cheap, high-count parts such as resistors, capacitors and LEDs, ordering 5% to 10% extra covers damage and assembly losses. For expensive parts, order closer to the exact number.
- **Check packaging.** SMD parts often come on reels or in tape cuts, while through-hole parts may come loose in bags. Tell the supplier how your assembly line takes parts.
- **Think about future batches.** If you will need the same part again in three months, ask whether the supply will stay consistent.

## Step 3: Order a Sample First

Before a large purchase, buy a small quantity and test it:
- Does it fit the footprint?
- Does it meet the electrical specification?
- Does it behave the same across several pieces?

## Step 4: Ask the Right Questions

When you request a quote, ask about:
- **Availability:** how much is in stock now, and how much would need to be arranged
- **Lead time:** how long a larger quantity would take
- **Consistency:** whether all pieces will come from the same batch
- **Brand and origin:** what make the parts are
- **Alternatives:** compatible options if the exact part is hard to get
- **Packaging:** loose, bagged, taped or reeled

## Step 5: Get the Paperwork Right

For a business purchase you will normally need:
- A **GST invoice** with your company name and GSTIN, so you can claim input credit where it applies
- A **clear quotation** listing each item, quantity and rate
- **Payment terms** agreed before the order, not after
- **Delivery details**, including who is responsible for transport

Keep the quotation, order and invoice together so you can trace any issue later.

## Step 6: Receive and Inspect

When the order arrives:
1. **Count it** against the invoice.
2. **Check the marking** on a sample from each lot.
3. **Run your incoming test** on a few pieces.
4. **Store the parts properly.** Use anti-static bags for ICs and keep moisture-sensitive parts dry.
5. **Report any problem quickly,** while the details are fresh and the invoice is recent.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Ordering by price alone.** The cheapest quote is not the cheapest if parts fail in production.
- **Vague specifications.** Missing tolerance, voltage rating or package causes returns.
- **Skipping the sample.** It is the cheapest insurance you have.
- **Mixing lots without checking.** Different batches may behave differently.
- **Forgetting the lead time.** A part that is "available soon" can stop a production line.
